On top of that, "Klecks" is a favourite name for children's characters and Kitas: the painting picture book *Kritzl & Klecks* (2015, nominated for the Deutscher Jugendliteraturpreis), *Carli und Klecks*, *Hase Klecks*, and countless "Kita Klecks". That makes the bare word warm and familiar, and it's also why nobody can own it, us included. "Malspaß" has the opposite problem: it's a generic word, too weak to protect.

**Klecksinger is one coined word.** No register, store or website has it, and it is distinctive enough to register.

## 2. Why Klecksinger works

1. **It follows the family pattern exactly.** German verb + ‑inger, like glotzinger: *glotzen* → Glotzinger, *klecksen* → Klecksinger, "the one who makes splotches".
2. **It sounds like a friendly southern German surname** (Straubinger, Freisinger): warm, a bit folksy, not techy.
3. **It carries the mascot.** The app and the character reinforce each other, and a search for "Klecks" finds it.
4. **It can be protected.** That's the one thing neither "Malspaß" nor "Klecks" can offer.

### Watch‑outs

- **Pronunciation.** German: *KLEKS‑ing‑er*, with the "ng" as in *Finger*. English speakers may read *Kleck‑singer*. That's harmless, even fitting, since Klecks sings the colors. But they'll misspell it, so put **klex, kleks, klexinger** in the App Store keyword field.
- **Children don't need to say it.** They say "Klecks". Parents say "Klecksinger".
- **Use "Klecks" only as the character's name inside the app.** Never use it alone as a product, store or merch name. A plush toy sold as "Klecks" would run into sigikid's toy mark. Sell it as *Klecksinger* instead.
- **This is a register search, not legal clearance.** The DPMA doesn't check older marks when you file. Their owners can oppose within **three months after publication**. Because the closest marks are older "Klecks" marks in classes 16, 28 and 41, the safest first filing is **9 and 41 only**. Leave 16 and 28 until merchandise becomes real. If nobody opposes, the name is clear in Germany.
- **The family name "Appinger" isn't registered by anyone** (the register has only Eppinger, Happinger Hof and similar), including you. That's a separate decision, but worth knowing.
- **The seller shown in the store is "Stephan Isermann"** (individual account). "von Appinger" appears only in the app and on the website.

## 4. Branding

**Hierarchy:** *Klecksinger* is the product. *Klecks* is the friend inside it. *von Appinger* is the maker, named in the parents' area and on the website, never in the child's view.

- **Wordmark:** lowercase, rounded, hand‑lettered **klecksinger**, with **the dot on the i drawn as Klecks**, a drop. In the app the dot takes the color the child picks, just like Klecks does. That ties the logo, the mascot and the core mechanic together.
- **Icon:** Klecks's face, big, on paper white (`#fdfbf7`, the app's own background) with one splash and **no text**. Children recognise faces. Test two or three variants with the App Store's Product Page Optimization (alternate icons have to ship in the build).
- **Colors:** paper white plus the app's ten paint colors. Light, not the dark neon of noisinger and burpinger. It sits naturally next to babinger's light pastels, which gives the parents' apps a shared look.
- **Sound:** Klecks's voice is as much the brand as the logo. A three‑ or four‑note motif from the pentatonic paint tones can be the sound logo (app start, store video).
- **Tagline:** DE *Die Mal‑App, die selbst Schluss macht.* · EN *The painting app that knows when to stop.*
- **Three things every piece of copy says:** it ends kindly · it's for making, not consuming · pay once, no data.
