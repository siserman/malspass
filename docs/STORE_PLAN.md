## 1. Competitive analysis

### Who's there

| App | Maker | Model | Price | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drawing for Kids / Malen für Kinder** | Bimi Boo (UAE) | free + one‑time unlocks | **Full version $11.99**, themed packs $2.99 | The category leader: **638K ratings in the US**, ~74K in Germany. It sells a one‑time full version, not a subscription, so **a one‑time unlock works at scale in this exact category.** Its privacy label lists coarse location, device ID and usage data for analytics, plus "other data" for marketing |
| **Zeichen‑Akademie / Malen für Kinder: Spiele ab 3** | Bini Bambini Academy | free + subscription | **€7.99/week**, €6.99/month, €39.99/year | Typical of the aggressive end. It has a play‑time limit, but only as a parental setting. Its label lists an email address linked to the user, plus analytics |
| **Pok Pok** | Pok Pok | free + subscription | $6.99–9.99/month, $45.99–59.99/year, **lifetime $89.99** | The premium "calm" benchmark. Proves parents pay a lot for calm, Montessori‑style play |

Toca Boca went the same way as Sago: it merged its paid apps into a free‑to‑play world with paid unlocks in 2018.

### What the table says

1. **The open position is the ending, not the limit.** Time limits exist (Bini has one, and iOS 27 has per‑category allowances). Nobody makes a kind, character‑led *ending ritual* the core of a painting app. Nighty Night shows the ritual sells, in a different genre.
2. **"Pay once" separates Klecksinger from the subscription players.** €39.99 a year (Bini), $45–60 a year (Pok Pok) and $7.99 a month (Sago) set the comparison. A single €6.99 purchase is the honest alternative.
3. **"No data" separates it from the leader.** Bimi Boo's privacy label lists location, device ID and usage data. Klecksinger's label will be **"Data Not Collected"**, which shows on the store page before anyone downloads.
4. **Nobody else combines** spoken colors in German and English, an exact time‑lapse of every picture, real pigment mixing and tools that arrive over days.
5. **The threat is volume.** A search for "Malen Kinder" brings up dozens of apps, some with tens of thousands of ratings. Klecksinger can't win that search organically. It has to be found through people: press, awards, Kitas, pediatricians and parent communities.
6. **Copying is cheap.** Any feature list can be cloned in a week now. What can't be cloned quickly is Klecks, a voice parents recognise, awards and reviews. Those are where the time should go after launch.

## 2. Paid or free — the decision in detail

| Model | For | Against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid upfront (€4.99–6.99) | Cleanest trust signal ("no in‑app purchases"); no purchase code | Few downloads, so few ratings; no trial; Fiete needs constant promotions to make it work | No |
| **Free + one‑time unlock** | Trial; ratings; one purchase, not a subscription; the category leader proves it works; Family Sharing | Shows "In‑App‑Käufe" on the store page, so the listing has to say plainly: *einmal kaufen, kein Abo, keine Werbung* | **Yes** |
| Subscription | Recurring revenue | Breaks the promise; can't justify a monthly fee for 30 minutes on some days; families churn as children age out | No |
| Entirely free | Most goodwill and downloads | No revenue for a product that costs voice work and weeks of time; the portfolio wants to test buy‑once pricing (CLAUDE.md: "each app gets a buy‑once option") | No |

**Price: €6.99.** The concept said €5.99. Bimi Boo's $11.99 full version shows there's room, and €6.99 stays clearly below it and inside impulse range. After 19 % VAT and the 15 % Small Business rate, that is **≈ €4.99 per sale**. Until Apple approves the Small Business Program (still pending), the rate is 30 % and the net is **≈ €4.11**.

**Where the free part ends.** I'd draw the line a little earlier than the concept does:
- **Free:** chapters 1–2, Malkasten levels 1–3, the fridge with time‑lapse, and **every safety feature**. Safety is never paywalled.
- **Klecks' ganze Welt:** chapters 3–6, Malkasten levels 4–7, film export, and every future chapter (7–12) as free updates.

The concept's line (chapters 1–3 plus the whole Malkasten free) gives away the part children use every day, and leaves little to buy.

**The child never sees a purchase.** Apple's Kids Category rule 1.3 allows purchases only behind the parental gate. Locked chapters therefore don't appear as locked gifts in the child's world. They simply aren't there yet. Parents learn about the unlock in two places: the first‑start note ("Zwei Kapitel sind frei. Die ganze Welt: einmal 6,99 €, kein Abo") and one calm card in the parents' area. That costs some conversion, and it's the right call.

**The Appinger Pass:** not in version 1. The Pass runs through RevenueCat, and every third‑party SDK is a risk in the Kids Category. Revisit it once the Pass exists and can unlock Klecksinger without adding an SDK.

### What the numbers mean

- **Cash break‑even:** about 20–30 sales cover a month of a paid ElevenLabs plan and Google's one‑time fee. The Apple membership is already paid for the portfolio.
- **1,000 sales ≈ €5,000.** If 2–5 % of downloads buy, that takes **20,000–50,000 downloads**, which a small DACH kids app doesn't get without being featured, winning an award or getting press.
- **An honest outlook:** a few hundred to a couple of thousand sales in year one. The bigger value is strategic. It's a second app for babinger's audience (parents), it carries the same trust story (local, no data, no dark patterns), and it's the portfolio's cleanest test of buy‑once pricing.

## 3. Is it worth it at all?

**For:**
- **The prototype is mostly built.** 5,300 lines, six chapters, the Malkasten, sessions, the fridge, backup, DE/EN. Shipping is mostly packaging, not building.
- **It's differentiated on the one axis the competition can't copy without changing its business:** an app that ends by itself can't be sold by subscription or ads.
- **It shares babinger's audience and stance**, so marketing for one helps the other.
- **The German press channel exists:** iphone‑ticker, ifun and appgefahren regularly write about ad‑free kids apps.

**Against:**
- **The queue.** The portfolio rule is one app at a time, and babinger is next. Since 2026-10-12 Klecksinger is in the portfolio table and on the master board (*20 Next In Line*), after babinger; whether it goes before or after workinger2 is still open.
- **The money is small**, and the time budget next to a full‑time job is the real cost.
- **Germany is blocked right now.** Until the DSA trader document is verified, no app from this account sells in the EU, and Klecksinger is German‑first. (Switzerland isn't in the EU, so it would work there.)

**Verdict:** ship it, **after babinger**. My suggestion is ahead of workinger2, because Klecksinger is closer to done (workinger2 still needs an iOS target), it shares babinger's audience, and it tests buy‑once pricing. That's your call. The order should be decided on the master board, not by whichever app gets worked on.

## 4. What it takes — the checklist

### A. Account and legal (blockers)
- [ ] **DSA trader verification** (the name document): without it, no sales in Germany or Austria. It's account‑wide and already gates babinger
- [x] **Voice licences:** the Piper clips (built on non‑commercial data) are gone, replaced 12 Oct 2026. The new recordings are on a paid plan; check which plan made the ten German color clips in August
- [x] **All 60 lines recorded in both languages** (12 Oct 2026, ElevenLabs *Laura*), replacing the Piper clips. They were made on a **paid** plan (confirmed), so ElevenLabs licenses them for commercial use; keep the invoice. A voice actor remains an option for the final Klecks
- [ ] **Name: Klecksinger** (see [NAME_AND_BRAND.md](NAME_AND_BRAND.md)). "Malspaß" is too generic to protect, and "Klecks" alone is taken in our classes (sigikid for toys, a childcare provider for education, "Kleo & Klecks" for software). "Klecksinger" turns up nowhere: no app, no trademark, no domain. Reserve it in App Store Connect and file it at the DPMA
- [ ] **Privacy policy and support page** on appinger.app: a short Klecksinger section ("no data collected; drawings stay on the device")
- [ ] **Licence list** for fonts, sounds and icons (start it now)

### B. Product, for version 1
- [ ] **Version 1 = what the prototype has.** Six chapters, seven Malkasten levels, the SVG Klecks. Rive and chapters 7–8 come later, paid for by sales
- [ ] **Family playtest:** 5–10 families for two weeks (concept phase 0). Do goodnights go smoothly? Do 3‑year‑olds hit the 1.4 cm tool buttons? Does a resting wrist change colors? The PWA works for this today, with no code
- [ ] **The free/paid line** (section 2) and the parents' first‑start note with the price

### C. The native app (Capacitor)
- [ ] **Capacitor 8 shell**, iOS first. It needs iOS 15 or later, so the oldest hand‑me‑down iPads (stuck on iOS 12) are out. Xcode 27 is fine
- [ ] **Fridge storage from IndexedDB to files** (Capacitor Filesystem), with a one‑time migration. Children's drawings are the one thing that must never be lost, and WebView storage is not a safe place for them. Files are included in the iCloud device backup
- [ ] **Audio session "playback"**, replacing the MediaStream silent‑switch trick, and the clips as Web Audio buffers
- [ ] **Native share and save:** share sheet, Photos, and the ZIP backup (blob downloads don't work in a WebView)
- [ ] **Film export:** test MediaRecorder inside WKWebView. If it fails, encode natively with AVAssetWriter
- [ ] **StoreKit 2 non‑consumable** through a small plugin, **without RevenueCat**. Purchase and *Restore* sit only behind the parental gate, and Family Sharing is on. (Turning Family Sharing on for a product can't be undone)
- [ ] **`Mal.DEV = false`** in store builds, set by the build rather than by hand
- [ ] **App icon at 1024 px and a launch screen** (today's icons are 512 px PWA icons)
- [ ] **A release script** like the Flutter apps' `tool/release.sh`: `npx cap sync ios`, then xcodebuild archive and the altool upload with the same API key. The testflight‑release skill covers Flutter only
- [ ] **Test devices:** an old iPad on iOS 15, a current iPad and an iPhone, all in airplane mode

### D. Store listing and review
- [ ] **Kids Category**, age band **5 and under**. The new age‑rating questionnaire: parental controls yes, ads no, messaging no
- [ ] **Privacy label: "Data Not Collected"**
- [ ] **Screenshots** for 13" iPad and 6.9" iPhone in German and English. **An app preview video**: the time‑lapse makes one almost by itself
- [ ] **Listing text, DE/EN:** lead with *Die Mal‑App, die selbst Schluss macht* and *einmal kaufen, kein Abo, keine Werbung, keine Daten*. **No health claims** ("reduces tantrums"): the research belongs in the app's "why" section, not in store copy
- [ ] **Review notes:** how to pass the gate, that chapters open with the days, and how to open all of them in the parents' area so the reviewer sees everything. The first in‑app purchase must be submitted **together with** an app version
- [ ] **TestFlight** with the playtest families first, then submit
- [ ] **A featuring nomination** in App Store Connect for the Kids editors

### E. Launch (DACH)
- [ ] Press note to iphone‑ticker, ifun and appgefahren (they cover ad‑free kids apps)
- [ ] Submit for the **TOMMI** and the **Pädagogischer Medienpreis**; get listed on *klick‑tipps.net*
- [ ] Kitas and pediatric practices: a one‑page flyer
- [ ] Mention it alongside babinger in the same parent communities

### F. Android (later)
- [ ] Google Play Families (target audience 5 and under), Play Billing, $25 once, and test on a cheap Amazon Fire tablet. Then apply for *Teacher Approved*
